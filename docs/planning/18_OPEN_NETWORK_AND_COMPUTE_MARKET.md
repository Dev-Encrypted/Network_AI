# 18. Open participation and the optional compute market

## Open participation is the objective

Participants should be able to operate nodes, propose model profiles, inspect contracts, and choose compatible gateways. The network should support qualified community offers rather than a catalog controlled by a single model publisher.

Models above 27B are a recommended focus because larger profiles often require contributions beyond one person's equipment. Openness does not remove engine, memory, license, route, or trust requirements. A proposal for an arbitrary architecture needs an adapter and evidence before it becomes a service.

## Roles and decentralization

An inference node supplies work. A gateway provides access. A route operator coordinates the complete service. A ledger operator confirms accounting transitions. These are separate functions and may have different admission rules.

The historical ledger candidate is federated: four independent organizations, equal voting power, and a quorum of three. The current private implementation has one coordinator. Neither description should be presented as Bitcoin-style permissionless validation.

Portable identity, documented protocols, independent gateways and tested operator replacement are concrete advancement criteria. Multiple local processes are useful for testing but do not demonstrate organizational independence.

## Optional commercial offers

The cooperative TU system should not require cash buyers. A separate seller may offer a qualified route through a paid API when buyers and acceptable costs exist. One seller is responsible for the complete route toward the customer, including any funded subcontracted stages.

The offer must identify model/profile, seller, maximum price, unit, capacity window, measurement policy, finality, refunds, withdrawal, dispute responsibility and maximum loss. Commercial capacity cannot be double-booked against cooperative commitments.

## Separate money from TU

Track customer deposits, finalized service revenue, provider payables, fees, refunds and provisions independently. Unused deposits remain liabilities. An internal TU balance is not a cash reserve or an automatic withdrawal right.

A per-request maximum price does not authorize unlimited wallet funding. Funding and top-up caps need their own consent and accounting. Retries must not charge twice, and changing gateways must not strand an accepted customer's claim without a defined exit path.

## Candidate adapter and qualification

The original plan selected an x402 batch-settlement/EVM path on Base Sepolia with test USDC and a TypeScript SDK as an optional integration reference. This is a historical adapter selection, not a launched asset, a production deployment, or a required currency for TU. Exact release, contract, facilitator and operational terms still need qualification.

Test concurrent spending caps, persistent authorizations, replay, streaming/partial delivery, restart, reconciliation, disputes and withdrawal with the original gateway unavailable. Do not claim atomic settlement across unrelated chains or currencies without a demonstrated mechanism.

## Can the API be cheaper?

That is an objective to measure. Compare complete equivalent service: model revision, precision, context, output, quality, latency, completion and support terms. Subtract provider costs, infrastructure, payments, verification, failures and support before calling a price sustainable.

The private preview accepts no real payments and performs no payouts. [Commercial examples](19_OPEN_MARKET_SIMULATIONS.md) are hypothetical arithmetic; [FC04](../execution/GATES.md) remains open.
