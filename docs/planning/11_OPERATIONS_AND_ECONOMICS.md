# 11. Operations and complete costs

## A cooperative network still has real costs

No cash buyers does not mean free operation. Contributors bear or contract for electricity, hardware use, connectivity, storage and availability. Coordinators, verification, relays, artifact distribution, support, backups and replacement capacity also need a responsible funding source.

Separate cooperative TU circulation from external cash or in-kind resources. A TU balance cannot pay an electricity invoice unless a counterparty explicitly agrees to provide that resource under separate terms. Do not record an unconfirmed donation as secured runway.

## Plan cost by a qualified route

Larger models above 27B may require more devices and communication, but their real cost depends on the selected profile. Keep assumptions for weights, KV state, concurrency, loading time, readiness duration, redundancy, verification and network transfer distinct.

A nearby cluster might cost more per hour yet finish more requests. A cheaper slow route may miss its service objective. Compare completed equivalent service rather than only advertised GPU rental rates or parameter counts.

## Recurring cooperative coverage

Candidate v6 tests whether finalized recycled TU cover normal accepted readiness obligations during a mature phase without ordinary new issuance. Protected-reserve withdrawals, initial balances and extraordinary support must not be presented as recurring revenue.

The study also needs participant access: funds can appear healthy while contributors exhaust their spending balances or cannot reach their preferred model. Measure contribution, liquidity and funded demand by cohort. The F0 test failed this combined problem. [Economic results](../execution/ECONOMY_V1_RESULTS.md).

## Optional paid service

A seller's service margin must subtract provider obligations, payment processing, attributed infrastructure, support, refunds, failure losses and provisions from finalized service revenue. Customer deposits and TU balances are not revenue.

Compare a cheaper API only at equivalent model revision, precision, context, output, quality, latency and completion terms. There is no current qualified market price or demonstrated commercial margin in this repository.

## Routine operation

Monitor route readiness, queue age, completion, per-stage saturation, held balances, receipt delay, outbox backlog, abnormal usage, available funding and backup health. Logs should explain state transitions without routinely retaining prompts or secrets.

Updates need pinned artifacts, qualification, staged rollout, rollback and preservation of accepted sessions. An engine regression can alter memory or token counts even when endpoint names remain the same.

The private [operator guide](../implementation/OPERATIONS.md) documents owned processes, migration checksums, local status, backup and restoration. Those controls are not a public high-availability service.

## Hibernation and orderly exit

If a complete route, minimum liquidity, quorum, or external funding disappears, stop new affected promises, drain accepted work to its contract, reconcile obligations, and preserve balances and recovery data. A reserve provides bounded recovery time; it cannot promise automatic recovery when resources never return.

Before public opening, the historical candidate requires confirmed operating and closure funding, named responsible operators, and actual pilot evidence. These remain [open gates](../execution/GATES.md), with the detailed candidate in [chapter 24](24_CLOSURE_PROGRAM_AND_LAUNCH_GATES.md).
